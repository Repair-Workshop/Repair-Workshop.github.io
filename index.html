About the Bridge

Overview

Peer review is under growing pressure as rising submission volumes compound reviewer overload, uneven review quality, inconsistent standards, and bias. At the same time, large language models and AI tools are rapidly entering every stage of the scholarly workflow, from literature synthesis and manuscript preparation to claim checking and review drafting. While AI assistance can help reduce workload, it also introduces new risks, including unsupported criticism, overreliance, and blurred lines of accountability.

REPAIR (Rethinking Peer Review in the Age of Artificial Intelligence) is dedicated to developing peer-review systems that are more reliable, scalable, fair, and accountable. Rather than staging a competition between humans and machines, REPAIR brings together researchers and practitioners across machine learning, natural language processing, and information retrieval, human-computer interaction and computational social science, and research integrity, AI governance, and scientific publishing (including editors and conference organizers) to measure where human and AI judgment succeed, where each fails, and how carefully designed collaboration can strengthen peer review while preserving human responsibility.

Call for Participation

Topics of Interest

We welcome technical, empirical, human-centered, governance, and system-building contributions across four interconnected themes (as well as related topics):

Theme 01
AI-Assisted Research and Manuscript Preparation

How does the growing use of AI in literature synthesis, experimentation, and scientific writing change the object of review? Topics include authorship, provenance, disclosure, reproducibility, and evaluating scientific contributions.

Theme 02
AI-Mediated Reviewing and Human–AI Collaboration

Which forms of AI assistance strengthen expert judgment, and which introduce unsupported criticism, encourage overreliance, or obscure responsibility? Topics include AI-generated reviews, AI feedback for improving human reviews, and reviewer-support workflows.

Theme 03
Review-Quality Measurement and Evaluation

How should human and AI reviews be assessed for factual grounding, technical correctness, constructiveness, actionability, and fairness? Topics include claim-level verification, human–human and human–AI disagreement analysis, rubrics, datasets, and benchmarks.

Theme 04
Fairness, Accountability, and Governance in Peer Review

Under what conditions can AI-assisted reviewing remain trustworthy? Topics include bias, unequal access to AI tools, transparency and disclosure policies, confidentiality, manipulation and prompt-injection risks, and institutional accountability.

Featured Experiment

The REPAIR Living Lab

A shared review exercise using short, publicly available texts selected by the organizers, such as paper introductions, short papers, or research blog posts related to the bridge themes. Participants compare human and AI reviews of the same material and explore how AI feedback can improve their own reviews. Judgments stay limited to the claims and evidence in the supplied text. The exercise follows a Review–Audit–Repair model.

Stage 1
Participant reviews before the bridge

Participants who opt in independently review an assigned text before the event, without AI assistance, using a common template for strengths, concerns, and suggested improvements.

Stage 2
AI reviews prepared in advance

Organizers generate AI reviews of the same texts, with the same instructions and template, and without access to participants’ reviews.

Stage 3
Small-group review audit

During the bridge, small groups examine selected texts and their human and AI reviews, with identities and sources initially withheld, and record evidence for their judgments.

Stage 4
AI-assisted review improvement

Original reviewers examine AI feedback prepared in advance and accept, reject, or edit suggestions to produce a revised review, keeping responsibility for the judgment.

Stage 5
Evaluation of revised reviews

Other participants compare original human, AI, and revised human reviews with a shared rubric for factual support, constructiveness, actionability, and coverage.

Stage 6
Review Court

Selected disagreements are examined with the wider audience. Groups present the evidence, possible resolutions, and remaining uncertainty.

Participation and data handling. Joining the review and evaluation exercise is voluntary and subject to consent, ethics, and confidentiality procedures. Texts and reviews will not be used to train models as part of the exercise. Participation in the Living Lab is not required to attend the bridge.

One-Day Bridge

REPAIR is a one-day, in-person bridge. The activities below are confirmed. A timed schedule will be posted once the order of sessions is set.

Activity
Education and tutorials

Researchers and practitioners discuss AI-assisted reviewing, reviewer behavior, review-quality assessment, and perspectives from editors and conference organizers.

Activity
REPAIR Living Lab

Small groups audit participant and AI reviews prepared before the event, revise reviews using AI feedback, and discuss selected disagreements in a moderated Review Court.

Activity
Panel discussion

Researchers, conference organizers, and practitioners discuss AI’s roles in reviewing, accountability, and the evidence needed to guide peer-review practice. Panelists TBA.

Lunch
Posters and tool demonstrations

A one-hour lunchtime session for posters on REPAIR-related research, including ongoing work, and live demonstrations arranged with the organizers in advance.

Activity
Community discussion

The bridge closes by identifying shared research priorities, opportunities for joint studies and shared resources, and next steps for continued collaboration.

Call for Participation

Submission Tracks and Guidelines

We invite submissions in three formats. All page limits exclude references.

Submission Type Page Limit (excl. references) Scope
Extended Abstracts Up to 2 pages Early-stage ideas, preliminary findings, position statements, or ongoing work
Research Papers (Short) Up to 4 pages Focused empirical studies, methods, benchmarks, or analyses
Research Papers (Long) Up to 8 pages Full-length technical, human-centered, or governance research contributions
Demo Tools Up to 4 pages + 2-minute video Peer-review systems, reviewer-support tools, claim-verification pipelines, or evaluation interfaces (for live demonstration during the lunchtime session)

Presentation Preference (Poster or Talk)

Every accepted submission (across extended abstracts, short/long research papers, and demo tools) may be presented as either a Poster or a Talk. Applicants will choose their preferred presentation format (Poster, Talk, or Either) when submitting on OpenReview.

Formatting, Review, and Archival Policy

  • Template: Submissions must be in PDF using the AAAI-27 two-column format (AAAI-27 Author Kit: https://aaai.org/authorkit27/).
  • Review Process: Extended abstracts and research papers (short and long) will undergo double-blind peer review; please ensure your PDF is fully anonymized (omit author names, affiliations, acknowledgments, and identifying links). Demo tool submissions do not need to be anonymized (single-blind review).
  • Demo Tool Video: Demo submissions must also include a 2-minute video demonstrating the submitted tool.
  • Non-Archival: All tracks are non-archival (no formal AAAI proceedings). Ongoing, concurrent, or previously published work is welcome, and authors retain full rights to submit or publish their work elsewhere. With author consent, accepted contributions may be linked or posted on the REPAIR website.
  • In-Person Attendance: Per AAAI-27 Bridge Program policy, all Bridge presentations must be delivered in person (virtual presentations are not permitted). At least one author of each accepted submission must register for the AAAI-27 Bridge Program and attend in person.
