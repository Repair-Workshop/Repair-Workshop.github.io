About the Workshop

Peer review is under increasing pressure as major AI conferences operate at a scale that makes consistent quality, calibration, and fair decision-making difficult to sustain. AAAI submissions, for example, grew from approximately 15,000 in 2025 to 30,000 in 2026. At the same time, LLMs are reshaping both how manuscripts are produced and how reviews are written. REPAIR 2027 brings together researchers from machine learning, NLP, information retrieval, HCI, computational social science, research integrity, and AI governance to develop peer-review systems that are more reliable, scalable, fair, and accountable in the age of AI.

A defining feature of the workshop is the REPAIR Living Lab: a prospective, instrumented study embedded in the workshop’s own peer-review process, comparing human, AI, and human–AI reviewing on the same real submissions.

Theme 01
AI-Assisted Manuscript Production

How the growing use of AI in scientific writing changes the object of review, including originality, authorship, provenance, disclosure, and responsibility when polished expression may obscure conceptual weakness or introduce unverifiable claims.

Theme 02
AI-Mediated Reviewing & Human–AI Collaboration

How AI may alter the process and quality of scientific reviewing. Which forms of assistance strengthen expert judgment, and which risk obscuring responsibility? The Living Lab compares independent AI review with human review and studies AI as a critic of already-written reviews.

Theme 03
Review-Quality Measurement & Evaluation

How the field should assess both human and AI reviews. Relevant dimensions include factual grounding, technical correctness, constructiveness, fairness, and other aspects that move beyond stylistic quality alone.

Theme 04
Fairness, Accountability, Legitimacy & Governance

Whether AI-assisted reviewing can remain trustworthy as these tools become more widely used. This includes bias, unequal access to AI assistance, transparency and disclosure, manipulation risks, and the conditions under which human responsibility can be preserved.

Featured Experiment

The REPAIR Living Lab

A prospective experiment embedded in the workshop’s own peer-review cycle. Rather than studying AI-mediated reviewing only through retrospective datasets or hypothetical scenarios, the Living Lab compares human, AI, and human–AI reviewing on the same real submissions and within the same review workflow. It follows a Review–Audit–Repair model: which review claims are factually supported; how human–human and human–AI disagreement compare; and whether AI can help reviewers improve completed reviews while retaining control over final judgments.

Stage 1
Independent human review

Each participating submission receives three independent human reviews, completed before reviewers see any experimental AI output. These reviews are frozen as the human baseline.

Stage 2
Shadow AI review

An AI reviewer independently analyzes the same paper and produces a structured claim ledger linking atomic criticisms to manuscript evidence, claim type, and confidence.

Stage 3
Blinded claim-level cross-audit

Evaluators assess randomized human and AI review claims without knowing their source, judging factual support, correctness, importance, actionability, and appropriateness.

Stage 4
Human–AI review repair

The AI critiques completed human reviews by flagging unsupported, vague, inconsistent, or incomplete comments. Reviewers may accept, reject, or edit each suggestion.

Stage 5
Independent outcome evaluation

Human, AI, and Human+AI reviews are evaluated for factual grounding, technical correctness, constructiveness, coverage, and usefulness, emphasizing complementarity.

Protection of the official review process. Acceptance decisions remain based solely on the standard human review process; all AI outputs operate as a shadow evaluation track. Participation is voluntary and subject to ethics and confidentiality procedures. Manuscripts and reviews are excluded from model training. Participation in the Living Lab is not required for submission or acceptance.

Submissions

Call for Papers

We invite full papers, short papers, extended abstracts, demos, position papers, negative results, and early-stage work that study peer review as a technical, empirical, human-centered, or governance problem in the age of AI. We particularly encourage submissions that connect technical methods to real peer-review workflows, deployed systems, conference or journal practice, and empirical evidence, as well as work relevant to the REPAIR Living Lab. All accepted submissions will be non-archival and made available on the workshop website. Submissions are managed via OpenReview. Each paper receives three reviews. Participation in the Living Lab is not required for submission or acceptance.

Topics of Interest

We welcome papers on, but not limited to:

  • AI-assisted scientific writing and its implications for review
  • Authorship, provenance, and disclosure of AI-generated content
  • LLM-assisted reviewing, critique generation, and meta-review
  • Reviewer calibration and decision-support tools
  • Review-quality assessment, factual grounding, and constructiveness
  • Fairness, bias, and discrimination in peer review
  • Reviewer matching, auditing, and evaluation benchmarks
  • Human–AI collaboration and reviewer-support tools
  • Claim verification and evaluation of scientific judgment
  • Accountability and transparency of AI-assisted review
  • Manipulation risks and unequal access to AI tools
  • Policy and governance of AI use in scientific publishing
